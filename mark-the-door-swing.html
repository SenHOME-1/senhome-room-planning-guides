SenHOME Room Planning Guides

Draw the door swing before you move the wardrobe

Start with the hinge

Imagine a bedroom where the wardrobe fits neatly beside the door on a plan. In the room, the door handle hits the wardrobe before the door opens far enough to carry a laundry basket through. The floor plan had the right wall lengths. The overlooked detail was the arc of the door.

Mark the space that has to stay clear

Draw the wall and door opening on paper, or start with a measured plan. Mark the hinge on the correct side. Sketch the path of the outer corner as the door opens. Add the wardrobe at its measured depth, including the handles, and open its doors or drawers on the sketch too. A single rectangle for a closed wardrobe hides how much space it takes to use it.

Test one route in both layouts

Compare the original plan with one alternative that changes only the wardrobe position. Trace the same trip in both, from the hallway to the bed with the laundry basket. Check whether a person can put the basket down, open the wardrobe and close it again. You can mark the wardrobe footprint with paper tape in the actual room and walk that trip before moving heavy furniture.

Keep the useful measurement

If the second option works better, save the distance between the wardrobe and the door jamb along with the drawing. That gives you a measurement to check again when a skirting board, a handle or a different door thickness changes the fit.
