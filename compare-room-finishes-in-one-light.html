Compare two wall finishes in the same light

Hold the view steady

A soft green looked cooler in the second preview of a bedroom. The paint had changed, but so had the camera angle and the amount of window in the image. There was no way to tell which change was responsible. A useful comparison starts with one view you can reproduce.

Build a pair you can compare

Choose a wall where daylight and a shadow both fall. Save the original room view, including a piece of furniture you intend to keep. Duplicate that version. Change just the painted surface in the copy and keep the floor, curtains, camera position and lighting the same. Compare how each finish meets the skirting board and whether the edge of the furniture is still easy to see.

Check a second condition

Repeat the pair from the same position under the light you use after sunset. A finish that works well beside a bright window can read differently beside a warm lamp. Write down which combination you preferred and what you could actually see, such as the edge of a dark headboard disappearing against the wall.

Take the comparison back into the room

Use the previews to narrow your options. Place physical samples next to the furniture and look at them at the same times of day. The screen gives you a quick comparison, while the physical sample shows the texture, sheen and colour under your own lighting.
